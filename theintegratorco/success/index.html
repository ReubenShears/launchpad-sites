Cut from order to installation
Medical equipment distributor · Texas

An order that touched five teams and waited on all of them.

Every signed order crossed sales, engineering, logistics, field service and finance before an install date could be confirmed. Nobody owned the order end to end, so customers chased a status that no single person could give them, and the leadership team was seriously considering adding headcount to keep up.

We followed thirty live orders through the whole path and found most of the delay sitting in queues rather than in work. We collapsed two approval steps, gave each order a named owner, and connected the CRM to the scheduling tool so an install slot was reserved the moment an order was accepted.

  • Nine days removed from the average order to installation cycle
  • Status chasing calls largely eliminated, one owner per order
  • Planned headcount increase deferred, existing team absorbed the volume
1 source
Of truth, instead of four
Industrial services · Gulf Coast

Four systems, four versions of the same job.

Jobs were quoted in one tool, scheduled in a second, tracked in a shared spreadsheet and invoiced in a third. The same details were rekeyed at every step, invoices went out with the wrong scope often enough for it to be a running joke, and no report the leadership team looked at fully agreed with any other.

We mapped where each field originated, made the quoting system the single point of entry, and built integrations so scheduling and invoicing inherited the data rather than recreating it. The spreadsheet was retired deliberately, with its two genuinely useful views rebuilt as reports.

  • Duplicate data entry removed from three stages of the job lifecycle
  • Invoice disputes traced to rekeying errors effectively stopped
  • One weekly operations report that finance and delivery both trust
Week 1
New hires now productive from
B2B software & services · remote

Onboarding that lived entirely in one person's head.

Customer onboarding was run by a single long-serving operations lead. It worked, right up until she took leave and three accounts stalled for a fortnight. Nothing was documented, every new hire learned by shadowing, and the founder had quietly accepted that this was a person-shaped risk he could not remove.

We sat with her for a week, mapped every decision she was making without noticing, and rebuilt onboarding as a defined sequence with owners, templates and a checklist per account stage. Then we trained two other team members on it and ran a live handover with her on standby rather than in charge.

  • Onboarding fully documented, with an owner named at every stage
  • New team members contributing in their first week rather than their second month
  • Key-person risk removed from the most customer-facing process in the business
