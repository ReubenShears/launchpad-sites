What we do

Assess the movement, not just the sore spot.

Every plan starts with watching how you actually move under the demands of your art form, then building the strength and control the choreography needs.

◎

Injury assessment and rehabilitation

A full evaluation of the injury and of the mechanics that produced it, followed by active rehabilitation that keeps you moving rather than sidelined for the whole season.

◈

Movement and biomechanics screening

A detailed look at alignment, turnout, ankle and hip control, landing mechanics and load tolerance, so we find the limitation before it becomes an injury.

✦

Return to performance programming

Strength, resilience and recovery capacity built around your rehearsal calendar, so coming back is a staged plan rather than a hopeful first day back in class.

Questions

What performers ask before they book.

Do I have to stop dancing?

Rarely, and only when the tissue genuinely needs it. In most cases we modify what you do rather than remove it, because complete rest usually costs you more conditioning than the injury did.

Do you only work with dancers?

Dancers and performing artists are the core of the practice, and the same approach suits singers, actors, gymnasts, cheer athletes and anyone whose body is the instrument they perform with.

What happens on the free introductory call?

We talk through your history, your symptoms and what you are training toward, and you get an honest view of whether this is something we can help with before you spend anything.

How old do you have to be?

Young dancers are welcome and are a large part of the practice. A parent or guardian is asked to attend the evaluation so that everyone understands the plan and the timeline.

How long does it usually take?

It depends entirely on the injury, the season you are in and the demands of your repertoire. You will be given a realistic timeline at the evaluation rather than an optimistic one.
