The story

I came to coaching from the other side of the scan.

Before this, I worked in nuclear medicine. That job puts you in a room with people at the exact moment the abstract becomes real: the scan is on the screen, the number is what it is, and the years of meaning to get around to it are over. I saw a lot of capable, successful men have that moment in their forties and fifties. Almost none of them had been lazy. They had been busy, which is a different thing and a far more dangerous one.

So I went and got certified as a strength and conditioning coach and started training the people I had spent years watching arrive too late. What I found is that the standard advice fails them for a specific reason: it is written for someone with time and an undamaged body. Give a 46 year old managing partner a six day split and a meal prep plan and you have not helped him, you have handed him one more thing to fail at.

The Executive Rebuild Method is what came out of that. Assess first so we are not loading a broken pattern. Three sessions a week, because three sessions that happen beat six that do not. Ninety days with real checkpoints, so it ends with evidence instead of a vague feeling. It is deliberately unglamorous and it works for the man it was built for.

How I work

Three things I will not compromise on.

▣

Assessment comes first

No client trains before I have seen how he moves. Loading a restricted hip or a locked up thoracic spine does not build strength, it builds the injury that ends the program in week six.

◆

The plan bends, you do not break

Board meetings run long, flights get moved, kids get sick. When the week falls apart we rebuild the week, not your motivation. Consistency over ninety days beats intensity over nine.

◐

I turn people away

If you need a physician or a physical therapist before you need a coach, I will say so on the first call and help you find one. A client I cannot get results for costs me more than he pays.
