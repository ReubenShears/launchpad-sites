Day One
Newly diagnosed, nothing making sense
Newly diagnosed

From total shock to a plan he could follow

Mickey came to us shortly after diagnosis, in the stage most people describe as a fog. He had been handed leaflets, a meter and a follow up appointment months away, with no idea what to do on the Monday morning in between.

We started with the free DD-Diary and one instruction: log honestly, judge nothing, for two weeks. With a fortnight of real days on the screen, his coach could show him the handful of routines driving most of his numbers, and they changed one at a time.

  • Two weeks of logging replaced guesswork with a picture he could actually read
  • His coach translated the charts into three specific changes, not thirty
  • He describes the difference as clarity, confidence and a plan
90 Days
Type 2, from lost to in control
Type 2 coaching path

He was not short of effort. He was short of direction.

After his Type 2 diagnosis, Jason felt lost. He was trying, cutting things out, adding things in, without any way of knowing which of it was working. Effort with no feedback loop is the fastest route to giving up.

On the 90 day path he worked weekly with Coach Tyler. They read the diary together, found the patterns behind the spikes, and rebuilt his week around the routines that held up on busy days rather than perfect ones.

  • Weekly sessions turned scattered logging into a repeatable weekly rhythm
  • Nutrition and timing changes were tested against his own data, not generic advice
  • He now talks about reversing his diabetes as something possible, not wishful
180 Days
Years in, stuck, and finally consistent
Long term member

Consistency that survived a bad month

Some members have lived with diabetes for decades. They know the theory inside out. What they have lost is the belief that anything will stick, because every previous run of good weeks eventually collapsed under a busy month.

The 180 day path is built for exactly that. Long enough to hit the holidays, the work crunch and the week everything goes wrong, with a coach still there afterwards. Streaks, points and badges do the quiet work of making the restart feel routine instead of shameful.

  • Habits were rebuilt to survive disruption rather than depend on perfect weeks
  • Setbacks became a coaching conversation instead of a reason to quit
  • Six months in, logging is automatic and the plan belongs to them, not us
