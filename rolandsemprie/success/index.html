Fully booked, barely profitable, and out of hours to sell.

Deanna had a full roster and nothing left in the week. Every solution she had been offered involved finding more clients, which was the one thing her calendar physically could not absorb. The effort was not the problem. The standards around what her work was worth were.

We left the client count alone and rebuilt the offer, the pricing and the decision framework behind both. Then we moved the delivery hours to where her energy actually was, which meant fixing the training and the sleep before touching the calendar.

  • Rates doubled across the roster over two renewal cycles
  • Same number of clients, four fewer working hours a week
  • First quarter in three years she did not consider quitting

Agency founder · Toronto

He had bought three programs in two years and finished none of them.

Marcus was running a growing agency on five hours of sleep and whatever was closest at lunch. He had decided the problem was discipline, which is the conclusion almost everyone reaches and almost nobody is right about.

We started with the self-negotiation, not the program. Three non-negotiable sessions he could not argue his way out of, food decisions made once instead of three times a day, and a hard standard on which calls were allowed to touch his mornings.

  • Training four mornings a week by the end of the first quarter
  • Sleeping seven hours on a normal week for the first time in years
  • Two low-margin retainers cut, revenue up on fewer accounts

Executive · Toronto

She had run out of energy long before she ran out of ambition.

Ray came to me for weight loss. The weight was real, but underneath it was a decade of putting herself last on a list she had written herself, and a level of mental fatigue she had stopped noticing because it had become normal.

We reframed the identity first, because nothing else would have held. Then strength training, a nutrition strategy built for travel weeks, and a weekly review that made drifting standards visible before they became a pattern.

  • Down two dress sizes and holding it a year and a half later
  • Strength training three times a week, including on the road
  • Took the promotion she had turned down twice before
