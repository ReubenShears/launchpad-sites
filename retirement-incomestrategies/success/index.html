How the work runs

Three households, three very different problems.

Income
A monthly figure they could finally plan around
Retiring in 18 months

A couple with three accounts and no idea what they added up to

They had a 401(k) from a long career, an old IRA nobody had looked at in years, and a pension statement neither of them could read. What they wanted was simple: a number they could count on every month once the paychecks stopped.

We mapped every source of income they had, including Social Security timing, then set aside a portion of their savings to create protected lifetime income and left the rest invested for growth and access.

  • One page showing exactly what arrives each month, for life
  • A protected base of income that a market drop cannot reduce
  • The remaining savings kept liquid for travel and the unexpected
Protected
Savings taken out of the path of the next downturn
Federal employee, TSP

Thirty years of federal service, and one very exposed balance

He had done everything right for three decades and had a substantial TSP balance to show for it. What worried him was timing: retiring into a bad market year, drawing income from a falling account, and never getting the ground back.

We walked through his federal benefits alongside his own savings, then moved part of the balance into a contract with no exposure to market losses, so his core income no longer depended on what the market did in any given year.

  • Core retirement income insulated from market volatility
  • Federal benefits and personal savings finally read as one plan
  • A written distribution order, so he knows which account to draw first
Legacy
What passes on, planned rather than left to chance
Already retired

A widow who wanted her grandchildren looked after properly

Her income was comfortable. What she had never addressed was what happened next: how much of it would reach her family, what an extended care event would do to it, and whether either question had a better answer than the current one.

We reviewed her income needs first and left them untouched, then restructured the portion she never expected to spend so that it works harder for the people she wants it to reach.

  • Legacy portion separated from money she may still need herself
  • A hybrid plan covering extended care, accessible if it is never needed
  • Her family able to explain the whole plan in a single conversation
