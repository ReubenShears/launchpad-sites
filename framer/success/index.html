In practice

Three teams, three very different sites.

4 days
Design signed off to site live
Seed stage startup

A launch page that could keep up with the pitch

The product story was changing every week as the team spoke to customers, but the website was a static build that took a developer half a day to touch. Anything they learned on a call took a fortnight to reach the homepage.

  • Rebuilt the marketing site on the canvas, with the design and the live site as one thing
  • Moved case studies and changelog into CMS collections the founders update themselves
  • Set up branches so a bigger repositioning could be built and reviewed before publishing
No dev
Needed to deliver client work
Design studio

Delivering finished sites, not just files

The studio designed beautifully and then subcontracted every build, which ate the margin and meant the final site rarely matched the mockup. Clients came back with edit requests the studio had to pass along and mark up.

  • Design and build now happen in the same file, so what is approved is what ships
  • Clients get a site they can edit themselves, with the studio kept on for the bigger work
  • Motion and interaction survive to production instead of being simplified in a rebuild
Same day
Campaign pages from brief to live
B2B marketing team

Campaign pages that ship while the campaign is still on

Every landing page went through a request queue, so a two week campaign lost its first week waiting for a page. Testing a second headline was theoretically possible and practically never happened.

  • A reusable page structure the team duplicates and fills in for each campaign
  • Titles, descriptions and social previews set at design time, so nothing launches unoptimized
  • Copy changes published in seconds, which made testing variations a normal part of the week
