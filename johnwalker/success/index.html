Case studies

Three businesses, three different bottlenecks.

Website
Rebuilt around one clear action
Home services

A site with plenty of visitors and almost no enquiries.

A Treasure Valley home services company was getting steady traffic from search but hardly any calls. The site listed everything they did across nine pages, buried the phone number, and gave a visitor no obvious next step on any of them.

We cut it back to the three services that actually paid, rewrote the homepage around the job a customer was trying to get done, put the phone number and a short enquiry form on every page, and fixed the load time on mobile where most of the traffic was coming from.

  • One clear action on every page instead of nine competing ones
  • Enquiry form and phone number visible without scrolling on mobile
  • Same traffic, a noticeably larger share of it turning into calls
Email
Follow-up that runs itself
Professional services

Leads coming in, then quietly going cold.

A professional services firm in Boise was generating enquiries but only following up when somebody remembered. Anyone who was not ready to buy that week disappeared, and the owner had no way of knowing how much work was walking away.

We built a simple automated sequence: an immediate acknowledgement, a short series answering the questions people always asked before committing, and a monthly newsletter to stay in front of the rest. Everything runs automatically, and the owner sees who is engaging.

  • Every enquiry acknowledged within minutes, not days
  • Slow burn leads nurtured without anyone remembering to do it
  • Enquiries from months earlier turning into booked work
Ads
Spend traced back to real customers
Retail and e-commerce

Advertising spend nobody could account for.

A small retailer had been running ads for over a year across two platforms with no tracking beyond the platforms' own dashboards. Both claimed credit for the same sales, the budget kept creeping up, and nothing in the reporting explained where growth was actually coming from.

We set up proper conversion tracking first so we could see the truth, then turned off the campaigns and audiences that were not producing, and moved that budget into the handful that were. The plan after that was straightforward: keep cutting the waste, keep scaling what works.

  • Every sale traced back to the campaign that produced it
  • Budget moved out of the ads that were quietly burning it
  • A monthly report the owner can read in five minutes
