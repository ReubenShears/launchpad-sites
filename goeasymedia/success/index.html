Case studies

Three operators, three very different problems.

2.4×
More booked rides in 90 days
Executive black car

Midweek fleet sitting idle, weekends stacked

A twelve car executive fleet was profitable on paper and painful in practice. Weekends were full, Tuesday to Thursday the cars barely moved, and the existing campaign was one broad match ad group pointed at the homepage.

We split the account by service and by intent, built a dedicated corporate travel campaign scheduled around business hours, and pointed airport searches at a page that answered the two questions every corporate booker asks: meet and greet, and flight tracking. Call tracking went in on day one so every inquiry could be traced.

  • Booked ride inquiries up 2.4 times inside the first quarter
  • Midweek utilization lifted enough to justify two additional chauffeurs
  • Cost per booked ride down by just over a third

Weddings and events

Plenty of inquiries, almost none worth quoting

An events focused operator was drowning in calls and closing almost nothing. The search term report told the story in about four minutes: they were paying for people looking for taxis, rideshare, limo driver jobs and rental prices in a city three states away.

We rebuilt around exact and phrase intent, wrote a negative list from the actual terms rather than a template, and separated wedding, prom and party bus into campaigns with their own budgets and their own landing pages. Ad copy started leading with package pricing to filter out the bargain hunters before the click.

  • Cost per booked ride cut by 46 percent within two months
  • Wasted spend on irrelevant searches down to near zero
  • Quote to booking rate roughly doubled as inquiry quality rose
3 mkts
Opened without extra headcount
Multi market group

Growth stalled because nobody could prove what worked

A group running airport and corporate work in two cities wanted to expand but could not justify the risk. The account mixed both markets into shared campaigns, so no one could say which city was actually carrying the spend.

We separated the markets completely, gave each its own budget, tracking and reporting, and ran a controlled test in a third city with a small ring fenced budget before committing. Once the numbers held for six weeks, we scaled it.

  • Third market opened profitably without adding office staff
  • Clear cost per booked ride reported per city, every month
  • Budget shifted month to month toward whichever market performed
